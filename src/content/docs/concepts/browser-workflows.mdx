Many of the systems your team works in have no API: a supplier portal, an
older records system, a site you sign in to and click through. A **browser
workflow** teaches your agents one of those sites once. It is a
[connector](/concepts/connectors) whose **tools** are not API calls but
written procedures — each one a [skill](/concepts/skills) with its
instructions and scripts — that the agent follows in its browser, on its own
computer.

Two words are used throughout, and they are different things:

| Word | What it is | Where you meet it |
|---|---|---|
| **Browser workflow** | What someone builds once: its details, its variables and its tools | The **Browser workflow** tile in **Add a connector**, and the workflow's own page |
| **Connector** | What a company creates *from* a browser workflow, filling in its own values — then shares and grants like any connector | The Connectors list, the agent builder, a task's **Add a connector** |

One browser workflow can sit behind many connectors. The person who builds it
writes the steps; each company that uses it only says which site address (or
other value) is theirs.

## Building a browser workflow

Open **Connectors**. Under **Add a connector**, click the **Browser workflow**
tile — *Teach your agents a website once. Each tool is a skill they run on their
own computer.* It opens **New browser workflow**. The tile sits in the same grid
as the other connectors, just before **Custom MCP server** and **HTTP API**;
typing *browser* in the search box finds it at once. There is no menu or extra
step before it: the tile is the way in.

<Aside type="note">
  Building needs permission to add your own connectors, which owners, admins
  and members all have; without it the page says so and points you to an owner
  or admin of your workspace. On a workspace that holds regulated data,
  **Generate with agent** is not available: you write the tools yourself.
</Aside>

The page has the same shape as an agent's builder. The header carries the
actions — **Create browser workflow** on a new page; **Discard**, **Save** and
**Delete browser workflow** on a saved one — and an **Unsaved** chip shows while
anything on the page is not saved. A workflow has no draft or active state: it
is there until someone deletes it. A new page keeps everything you add — details, icon, variables,
tools and their scripts — until you press **Create browser workflow**, which
saves it in one go; if the create is refused, nothing you entered is lost.

| Section | What it holds |
|---|---|
| **Details** | The icon, **Name** and **Description**, as every company will see them |
| **Variables** | Values each company fills in when it creates its connector — usually the site address. Give each a **Label**, a **Key**, a **Kind** (**Text** or **Web address**), whether it is **Required**, and optionally an **Example** |
| **Tools** | What your agents can do on the site, one tool per job |
| **Connectors made from it** | The connectors created from this workflow that you can see. Open one to change it on the Connectors page |

### Variables

Anything that differs from one company to the next belongs in a variable, not
in a tool. Name it in braces inside a tool's instructions — `{site_url}` — and
each connector's own value is filled in when the agent loads the tool. A tool
written this way works for every company without being copied.

### Tools

**Add tool** opens the tool form beside the page — the same form as a
[workspace skill](/concepts/skills#writing-a-skill):

| Field | What it holds |
|---|---|
| **Name** | What the tool is called |
| **When to use it** | What the agent reads to decide whether to load it |
| **Instructions** | The steps the agent follows. Name a variable in braces, like `{site_url}` |
| **Scripts** | Code the steps run on the agent's computer, the same way every time — added with **Add script**, kept in folders |
| **History** | Every save is a version. **See what changed**, or **Restore** an earlier one |

A tool's row in the **Tools** list has **Switch off** and **Switch on**, and
**Remove**. A tool that is off is offered to no task from its next one;
removing a tool never disturbs a task that already loaded it. A connector can
be made from a workflow only while at least one of its tools is switched on;
until then only the people who can change it see its tile, and its panel says
*No tools yet, so nothing can be connected from it. Edit it to add one.*

A browser workflow's tools are theirs alone: they do not appear on the
**Skills** page or in the `/` menu, and an agent cannot change one from a task.

A tool can also carry [site calls](/concepts/site-calls) — the site's own
requests, sent from the agent's signed-in browser instead of clicking through
the page. A call's address can start with one of the workflow's variables, such
as `{site_url}`, so it works for every company's site.

### Generate with agent

You do not have to write the steps by hand. **Generate with agent** opens the
**Agent** tab: *Tell the agent which website to work with. It works in a
browser with you and proposes the tools when you are ready.* Explore the site
together, show it what to do with **Record** if you like, then ask it to
propose the tools. The agent can also turn steps into
[site calls](/concepts/site-calls), checking each one against what the site was
seen sending.

Nothing is saved until you approve. The agent's proposal arrives as a card
listing each tool — new ones marked, existing ones left as they are — and
**Approve** saves everything on it to the workflow at once; you can edit a
tool on the card first. Every company that uses the workflow gets the new
tools from its next task.

- **It is a session on a computer.** It spends AI credit like any task and
  follows the same rules as one when an allowance is used up.
- **One session per workflow.** A reload reattaches to it. If someone else
  started it you can follow along, but they work in it and end it.
- **Signing in stays out of the workflow.** When the site needs a login, the
  agent uses a sign-in already stored for you
  ([Personal sign-ins](/reference/settings/sign-ins)); no password is written
  into a tool.
- **New conversation** ends the session and keeps it as a task, which you can
  open again from **Session history**. Tools you approved stay saved; a
  proposal you did not approve does not.

### The icon

Choose the icon in **Details** by uploading one, taking one **From a website**
(the icon the site shows in a browser tab) or **Generate**. If you set none, one
is generated once, automatically, the first time the workflow has what it
needs.

## Using a browser workflow

Once a workflow has a tool switched on, anyone who can see it creates a
connector from it:
open **Connectors**, click its tile in **Add a connector**, and fill in:

- **Name** — optional; left blank, the connector takes the workflow's name.
- Its **variables** — for example **Site address**. Required ones are marked.
- **Who can use it** — the same share box as any connector.
- **Workspace** — only if you are in more than one workspace; your main one is
  chosen unless you pick another.

Then **Connect**. The tile reads **Browser workflow · Built by your team** or,
when AptiveAI published it to your company, **Browser workflow · From
AptiveAI**. On the workflow's own page, **Create a connector from it** on the
**Details** row does the same.

<Aside type="tip">
  The connector works like any other from here. Grant it to an agent in the
  builder under **Connectors** (all its tools, or some), or add it to one task
  with (+) or `@`. In the task the agent loads the tool it needs — the
  transcript shows it as a skill loading — with the connector's own values
  filled in and its scripts placed on the computer.
</Aside>

On the connector's panel:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Settings** | The variables' values. Saving them is checked: a missing required value or a web address that is not `http://` or `https://` is refused |
| **Tools** | Its tools, the ones its workflow offers |
| **Test connection** | Checks that the workflow is still there (not deleted) and available to your company, with a tool on and every required value filled in. It does not open the site |
| **Edit browser workflow** | Opens the workflow — only for whoever may change it |
| **Disconnect** | Deletes this connector and its settings. The workflow is not changed |

If a workflow gains a required variable, every connector made from it shows an
error naming the missing value until its owner fills it in, and tasks get none
of its tools meanwhile.

## Who can do what

| Action | Who |
|---|---|
| See a workflow's tile and create a connector from it | Anyone in a company that built it or that AptiveAI published it to, while it has a tool switched on and is not deleted |
| Change a browser workflow | The person who built it, or AptiveAI. Not another admin, and not the owner of the workspace; they see it read-only: *You can use this browser workflow, but you cannot change it.* |
| Delete or recover it | Whoever may change it |
| Publish it to other companies, or remove a deleted one for good before its 30 days are up | AptiveAI |
| Use and share a connector made from it | Its creator, and whoever it is shared with, as any connector |
| Change its settings, test or disconnect it | Its creator, and others who can edit it |

A workflow with no tool switched on is visible only to the people who can
change it.

## Deleting and recovering

Whoever may change a workflow deletes it with **Delete browser workflow** in the
header of its page, or with **Delete** on its tile's panel in **Add a
connector**, where **Edit** sits beside **Connect** too. The question says how
many connectors are made from it.

- **A deleted workflow stops working at once.** It leaves **Add a connector**,
  nobody can make a connector from it, an open session on it ends, and every
  connector made from it shows *No tools: the browser workflow this connector
  was made from is no longer available here.* Its tools are offered to no task.
- **It is kept for 30 days.** To find it, add **Deleted** in the search box of
  **Add a connector** (**Only**, or **With live**); its tile is dimmed and says
  when it is removed. It is read-only until someone who may change it presses
  **Recover**, which brings it back with every connector made from it.
- **After 30 days it is removed for good**, with its tools, their scripts and
  history, its grants to agents and its publications. The connectors made from
  it are not deleted — they belong to the people who made them — but they stop
  working for good.

**Training** is a working session with one of your agents, on its own
[computer](/concepts/tasks#the-live-view), where you teach it how a job is done
in your company — or improve how it does a job it already has. You lead, the
agent follows, and it keeps a list of what it has learned as you go. When you
are ready, you review that list and save the parts you want, as one new version
of the agent. **Nothing about the agent changes until you approve it.**

You can teach in any of four ways, and mix them in one session:

- **Tell it.** Explain the job in the conversation, the way you would to a new
  colleague — *"invoices over 60 days go to the finance lead, the rest wait
  for Friday"*. Talking is enough: the agent does not need a demonstration.
- **Give it files.** Attach a screenshot, a procedure, a price list or an
  export with the paperclip, or drop it onto the conversation.
- **Show it.** Press **● Record** and do the job once yourself on the
  computer; the agent reads the recording back.
- **Let it do the job while you watch.** Ask it to do the steps itself on the
  computer and correct it as it goes. Anything that writes to a system still
  stops on an approval card first.

The agent asks short questions in the conversation when something is not
clear, and uses a card only for a real choice.

| | Try it | Refine | Training |
|---|---|---|---|
| **For** | Checking how the agent works as it is | Telling the builder what to change in the agent's setup | Teaching the agent a job, or improving one, on its computer |
| **Who does the work** | The agent, as it would in a task | The builder changes the agent | You and the agent together, in a conversation on its computer |
| **What is saved** | Nothing — the conversation is not kept | An instruction change at once, as a new version; anything else on **Save agent** | Only the changes you tick and approve, as one new version you can undo |

[Try it and Refine](/concepts/agents#creating-an-agent) are described with the
agent builder.

## Starting a session

**From the agent.** Choose **Train**:

- in the agent's page header, beside **Share**, **Start a task** and
  **Clone**;
- in the agent's **…** menu on the Agents page or in the sidebar.

A computer starts — *Starting the computer…*, usually 20–40 s — and the
agent's page becomes the session: its conversation, and the side panel with the
**Live view**, **Details**, **Documents** and **Learned so far** tabs. The
agent's builder comes back when the session ends. Every training entry wears a
graduation cap.

**From a task.** Two ways lead from a task you are working on to training:

- **The agent offers it.** When it notices something worth keeping — you
  corrected it, or explained how something is done — the agent can ask on a
  **Training** card: *Teach this to ‹agent›?*, with what it noticed.
- **You ask for it.** **Train agent** in the task page's **…** menu asks the
  agent to make that offer. Its tooltip says *Teach what happened in this task
  to ‹agent›. The agent asks first.* It waits while the task has a question for
  you.

**Yes, open training** opens a session started with what happened in the task
— its first line reads *From your task “‹task›”: …*. **Not now** carries on
with the task and opens nothing. On a computer the session opens **beside the
task**, in a window of its own, and the task stays the page; on a phone the page
goes to the session, with **← Back to ‹task›** in its header. Both
conversations stay live. The task shows the session where you said yes, as a
card with the graduation cap and its state — *Training · Working*, *Needs you*,
*Open*, *Saved* or *Ended* — and the session's Save card stays in the
session. An **Approve all** you gave in the task never carries into the
training session. If you already have a session open on that agent, what the
task noticed is added to it rather than opening a second one, and the task
notes *Added to your open training of ‹agent›.*

Training is never offered on a task an [automation](/concepts/automations)
started, on a recording session or inside another training session.

## The session's computer

The session works on a computer like a task's, with a status pill over the
[live view](/concepts/tasks#the-live-view) that always says what is happening —
*Ready*, *Recording 01:42*, *Paused*, *Waiting for sign-in*, *The agent has a
question*. The **ⓘ** (*Show the details*) opens the three steps of a session,
marked **Done** and **Now**:

1. **Sign in, if the site asks** — *Use a stored sign-in or a new one. Nothing
   is captured while a sign-in page is open.*
2. **Teach it** — *Tell it in the chat how the job works, or press Record and
   do it once, then Stop recording. It tells you what it picked up. Repeat as
   often as you like.*
3. **Save** — *What it learns is listed under Learned so far. Press Save at the
   top to review it and approve all of it or some. Nothing changes until you
   do, and you can keep teaching after.*

The pill shrinks after a few seconds and, while you record, tucks up out of the
way (*Hide the controls*); rest the pointer on its small tab to bring it back.

**Signing in.** While a sign-in page is open the pill says *Waiting for
sign-in*. **Sign in with “‹name›”** uses one of the agent's own
[sign-ins](/concepts/agents#sign-ins); **Sign in with your “‹name›”** uses one
of your own [personal sign-ins](/reference/settings/sign-ins), as your own tasks
do. Your own sign-ins are offered only to you, in a session you started, and
are typed only on a turn you drive; anyone else in the session sees the agent's
only. **Sign in with a new account…** saves a new sign-in to the agent and uses
it. You can also sign in by hand in the live view.

**If no computer is free**, the pill says *No computer is free right now*, with
**↻ Try again** and **Discard session**.

The session's computer is not counted against the number of computers you may
hold at once, so opening training from a task never pauses the task, and the
session is not one of the tasks the agent
[works on at once](/concepts/agents#how-many-tasks-an-agent-works-on-at-once).

**Each session has a browser of its own.** It starts empty — so you sign in
again in every session — and it is never used by your tasks or by any later
session. When you end the session, its browser is emptied, sign-ins and all,
if its computer is still running then.

## Showing it: Record

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **● Record** | Starts recording what is done in the computer's browser |
| **❚❚ Pause** | Stops capturing for now — *nothing is captured while paused* |
| **● Resume** | Records again after a pause |
| **■ Stop recording** | Ends the recording and hands it to the agent |

The first time you press **● Record** in a browser, a warning says *This
computer is real*: *What you do on this computer is real: saving here changes
the record. Use one that's safe to change. While you work here, the agent sees
what the page sends to the site, except passwords, to copy it exactly.* — with
**● Record** and **Not yet**. Recording is not a practice run.

**What a recording keeps.** *The recording keeps the steps, the page addresses
and what the page sent to the site, never passwords or cookies.* Tokens, keys
and other credentials in what the page sent are hidden too. Recording pauses by
itself while a sign-in page is open (*paused while you sign in*) and carries on
when it is gone.

**The read-back.** When you press **■ Stop recording**, the conversation notes
*■ Stopped recording …* with its length, and the agent reads the recording and
tells you what it picked up. Correct it in the conversation if it misread
something. You can record as many times as you like in one session.

The agent can also start and stop a recording itself when you ask it to.

### What the agent sees of a site's requests

While you or the agent use a site, the agent can read the list of requests the
site's own page sent: each one's method and address, the **names** of its query
and body fields and headers, and the shape of each answer — not their values.
That is how it writes [site calls](/concepts/site-calls).

**A recording keeps the values.** When a step is recorded, the recording keeps
what the page sent with each request, values included — what was typed into a
field, what a hidden field carried — but never what the site answered.
Passwords, sign-in headers, tokens, keys and other credentials are hidden. So
when a call needs a value the page never shows, the agent reads it from a
recording of that step; with no recording, it asks you to record the step once.

## What training can change

| Change | What it does | Learned so far says |
|---|---|---|
| **A standing rule** | A short rule added to the end of the agent's instructions, true on every task | → instructions |
| **An instruction edit** | A larger change to the instructions. When what you ask for contradicts what is written, the new text replaces the old | → instructions |
| **A new skill** | How to do one kind of job, as one of [the agent's own skills](/concepts/skills#skills-an-agent-learns): its steps, and the scripts and [site calls](/concepts/site-calls) they use | → new skill |
| **A change to one of its skills** | Improves a skill the agent already has, under the same name | → its skill “‹name›” |
| **Conversation starters** | Adds or removes a [conversation starter](/concepts/agents#conversation-starters) | → starters |
| **Knowledge** | Adds a new [knowledge](/concepts/agents#knowledge) document, adds entries to the end of an existing file, or changes a passage in one | → knowledge |

**Knowledge** is for reference facts the job looks up — lists, prices, codes,
addresses, policies. A new document is written from what you said, pasted or
attached, never invented, as a `.md`, `.txt` or `.csv` file of up to 256 KB.
Only a text, Markdown or CSV file can be added to or changed; a PDF, Word file,
workbook or picture never is. In a training session the agent never writes to
its knowledge directly: it all waits for your approval like every other change.

**What training does not change.** The agent's connectors, guardrails, model,
name and sharing are not changed in training. When the agent thinks one of them
should change, it lists the idea under **Ideas, not saved** — *worth doing
yourself* — and you make the change in the agent's
[Configure](/concepts/agents#configuring-an-agent) tab if you agree. A
[sign-in](/concepts/agents#sign-ins) you add while signing in is saved to the
agent straight away, as it is anywhere else.

One set of changes holds up to 20 changes, of which up to five skills and five
new knowledge documents.

### Skill tests

The agent can test a skill it has drafted before you save it: it runs the skill
on the computer in a fresh context, the way a future task would, and the pill
says *Testing “‹skill›” · test 1 of 12* — *Watch it on the Live view*. A
session runs up to 12 tests in all.

A test acts for real in your systems, with one exception: a
[site call](/concepts/site-calls#skill-tests) that changes data is never sent in
a test. When a test is about to write — send an email, change a record — it
stops first and asks *Let tests in this session ‹do that›?*, with **Approve**
and **Decline**. **Approve all** given elsewhere in the session does not cover
a test. Once you approve a kind of write, the session's later tests may do it
too, and the conversation notes each one: *Done in a test, as you allowed for
tests in this session.*

## Learned so far

Everything the agent learns is listed on the **Learned so far** tab of the side
panel, one plain lesson per line with where it goes — *Always attach the PDF →
instructions*, *Close inspected events → new skill*. The chip **Learned so far ·
N** above the composer opens it. Ideas it cannot change itself are listed apart,
as *Ideas, not saved*.

The list is read-only. To change it, say so in the conversation — *"drop the
third one"*, *"the cut-off is 60 days, not 30"* — and the agent updates it.
With nothing learned yet the tab says *Nothing new to save yet.*

## Saving what it learned

<Steps>

1. **Press Save · N** at the top of the session — *Review what it learned and
   save it*. N counts the changes that can be saved; ideas never count. You can
   also just ask the agent to show you what it learned, in any words. The
   button reads **Preparing…** while the agent works.

2. **The agent reviews the whole session first.** Before it shows you anything,
   it goes back over everything you said, attached and showed, and everything it
   did, adds what it learned and had not yet listed, and corrects what the later
   conversation changed.

3. **Read the card.** It opens with *N changes ‹agent› learned* and shows each
   change in full: the rule, the instruction change as a before-and-after (lines
   starting with **-** are removed, **+** added), each skill's steps and files,
   the starters, and for knowledge *Adds the document “…”*, *Adds N entries to
   “…”* or *Updates “…”* with the text. Under **Changes** there is **one
   checkbox per change**, all ticked.

4. **Untick what you do not want, and answer.**

</Steps>

| Answer | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Approve** | *Save the ticked changes to the agent now. Training goes on.* The ticked changes are saved together, as one new version of the agent. **The changes you unticked are discarded** from the session and are not proposed again |
| **Keep training** | *Set this aside and go on teaching. Nothing is saved.* Every change stays on Learned so far |
| **Tell it what to change** | Type a correction instead of choosing. The agent revises the changes and shows a new card |

**Edit** on the card lets you fix a skill's **Name**, **When to use it**,
**Steps** or **Files**, or a starter's **Label** and **Request**, before you
approve. Nothing is saved to the agent until you approve.

Approve checks each change again as it saves: a change to something that was
edited since the agent staged it — by hand in Configure, or a knowledge file
someone uploaded again — is refused by name, and you untick it or ask the agent
to stage it again. If the agent has [automations](/concepts/automations), the
card says they use the changes from their next run.

**Training goes on after Approve.** The conversation notes *Saved as version
N.*, and how many unticked changes were discarded. You can keep teaching and
save again as often as you like; each save is its own version.

## Try it now

After a save, the top of the **Learned so far** tab offers **Try it now** —
*Starts a new task that uses only what was saved, and reports back here. It
asks before acting, like any new task.*

The try is an ordinary task of the agent, linked to the session: it runs on the
agent's saved setup and its own model, not on what the training conversation
knows, so it shows what the next real task will do. It starts with a conversation
starter — the one the session saved last, else the agent's first — and the
saved line says which (*Try it with: …*). With no starter at all it starts with
*Do the work you were trained to do, from start to finish.* On a computer it opens
beside the session; on a phone it opens as its page. It counts against your
computers like any task you start, and notifies you like one.

When the try ends, it reports back into the training conversation, and the
agent coaches on the result — and, if the try stopped to ask you something,
whether the saved setup should have covered it. A try that ran but had
nothing to work on says so — *It ran, but found nothing to work on, so this
doesn't show the steps work yet.* — rather than passing for a success. If a
skill went in untested,
the tab says *Not tested yet: ‹skills›. Try it once before you schedule it.*

<Aside type="caution" title="Try it now is real work.">
  The try acts in your systems like any task. It asks before every write, as a
  new task does: an **Approve all** you gave in the training session does not
  carry over to it.
</Aside>

## Undo

**Undo**, beside **Try it now**, takes back the session's latest save, while it
is still the agent's newest change. A confirmation, *Undo this training?*, says
exactly what will happen:

- the version the agent goes back to, with its instructions and starters as
  they were;
- skills the save added, *deleted for good*, and skills it changed, put back;
- knowledge documents it added, removed, and files it changed, put back as a
  new version;
- what is kept: a skill somebody edited since (kept unless you tick it), a
  skill with a locked version, a knowledge file changed again since.

*Anything tasks or automations already did with what this training saved stays
done.* Once the agent has changed after the save, Undo is no longer offered
there: *Use its Versions to go back* — see
[Versions](/concepts/agents#versions).

## Ending a session

**End training** in the session's header (or the computer's close button)
always asks first:

| The dialog | Its buttons |
|---|---|
| *Save what ‹agent› learned first?* — something learned is not saved yet | **Review and save** (the same as **Save · N**) · **Discard** · **Keep training** |
| *End this training session?* — nothing waits to be saved | **End training** · **Keep training** |
| *A recording is running* | **Stop recording first** · **End anyway** · **Keep training** |

**Discard** drops only what is not saved; anything you approved and did not
undo stays on the agent. Ending a session with a recording running deletes the
recording before the agent sees it. Ending can take up to a minute — *Ending the
training session…* — and the computer is closed.

A session also ends by itself after **a week without activity**. Its
conversation stays readable as a task, and what you saved stays on the agent.

## Leaving and coming back

A session stays open when you leave the page. Each person has **one open
session at a time**, across all agents:

- **Train**, the agent's **Configure**, or a link to the session brings you
  back to it, even after a reload. While your session on an agent is open,
  every way into that agent's builder lands on the session.
- Starting training on another agent while one is open is refused: *You're
  already training ‹agent›. End that session first.*, with **Open it**.
- An agent has one open session at a time. While a colleague trains it, **Train**
  says *‹name› has been training this agent since ‹time›.*

**Training history**, in the agent page's header, lists the agent's sessions.
Your open one opens the session; an ended one, or a colleague's, opens
read-only on its task page.

## Who can train

- **Anyone who may edit the agent and start tasks** — its creator, people
  given **Can edit**, the owners and admins of its workspace — a member on their
  own personal agent included. See [Sharing](/concepts/sharing).
- A session is a task, so it needs the same
  [paid access](/concepts/agents#paid-access) as starting one.
- **Not in a workspace that holds regulated data**: *Training is not available
  on this workspace: it holds regulated data.*
- Not on a deleted agent.

**Who is told.** When a session starts, the other owners of the workspace get a
notification, *A training session started on ‹agent›*, and every stored sign-in
typed during it is recorded in the audit log. See
[Inbox](/concepts/inbox#what-you-are-told-about).

A **schedule** starts a [task](/concepts/tasks) on its own at the times you
set — sweep the invoices every weekday at 7am, check a supplier portal every
hour, send a summary on Monday mornings. Each time it **fires**, a new task
starts on a computer in your workspace, works to the end, and appears in the
schedule's history like any other task.

Open **Schedules** from the sidebar. You can also ask an agent to set one up
for you in a task — see [From a conversation](#from-a-conversation).

## How it works

```
Cadence reaches its next time (e.g. weekdays at 7am, Europe/Madrid)
  → Gates checked: business hours, anything to process, AI credit
    → A task starts: the agent's instructions, then the schedule's
      → The agent works on its own computer until it is done
        → The firing lands in the schedule's History
```

If a check fails, the firing is **skipped** instead, with a reason, and the
schedule simply waits for its next time — see [Skipped firings](#skipped-firings).

## Creating a schedule

From the **Schedules** page, **New schedule** opens the form:

<Steps>

1. **Name** — what the schedule is called, such as *Morning invoice sweep*.

2. **Agent** — which [agent](/concepts/agents) does the work, or
   **No agent — free-form**. With an agent, its instructions come first and
   the schedule's instruction follows. Only active agents fire: a draft, paused
   or archived agent is shown as such in the list. The agent cannot be changed
   later — create a second schedule instead.

3. **What it does each time** — the instruction for every firing. Optional
   with an agent; required without one.

4. **When it needs your approval** — **Autonomous** (the default) runs to the
   end without asking anyone. **Interactive** stops and waits for you before
   sending, deleting, buying or submitting.

5. **Cadence** — **Hourly**, **Daily**, **Weekdays**, **Weekly** or
   **Custom cron**. New schedules start as Daily at 09:00. A custom cron has
   five fields (e.g. `0 9 * * 1-5`); anything more often than once a minute is
   refused. A preview shows the next time it will fire.

6. **Timezone** — stored on the schedule, so a 7am schedule means 7am where
   the work is, whoever edits it later.

7. **Gates** — optional checks before each firing (below).

</Steps>

You never choose a computer or what it is built from. Each firing gets a
computer in your workspace, chosen when it fires.

<Aside type="caution" title="Autonomous means nobody is asked.">
  An autonomous schedule approves every action for you — including ones that
  cannot be undone, and writes to your [connectors](/concepts/connectors) —
  at your own risk. Choose **Interactive** when the work should wait for a
  person before it sends, deletes, buys or submits; the waiting task then
  reaches you in the [Inbox](/concepts/inbox).
</Aside>

### Gates

| Gate | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Skip when there is nothing to process** | Looks first — in the place you describe under **Where to look**, such as *unpaid invoices older than 30 days* — and skips the firing if there is nothing to do |
| **Only fire during business hours** | Skips firings outside **From** – **Until** on the days you pick. Defaults to 09:00–17:00, Monday to Friday |
| **Pause after repeated failures** | Pauses the schedule after a number of **Consecutive failures** in a row (5 by default, 1–100) |

## The Schedules page

The top of the page counts **Total**, **Enabled**, **Running** and
**Failed (24h)** schedules, and shows when the **Next task** starts. Below it,
two views:

- **List view** — schedules grouped by agent, each with its **Status**,
  **Cadence**, **Next** time and which computer it uses. The default, and the only view
  on a narrow screen.
- **Timeline view** — every schedule as a lane, with past and upcoming firings
  along it, over **12h**, **24h**, **3d**, **7d** or **15d**. Click a firing to
  open its task, answer it, open its live view or edit the schedule.

The view you pick is remembered in this browser.

Each schedule's menu offers **Run now**, **Pause** / **Resume**, **Edit** and
**Delete** to the people who may change it. **Run now** fires immediately,
through the same gates as a timed firing, without moving the next scheduled
time; a paused schedule must be resumed first. **Delete** removes the schedule
and its whole history, and cannot be undone.

### A schedule's page

Opening a schedule shows its **Cadence** card (agent, mode, state, cron,
timezone, next and last firing), **What it does each time**, its **Gates**,
and its **History**: every firing with when it **Started**, its **Outcome**,
how long it **Took** and any detail such as a skip reason. Open a firing to see
what it was asked to do at the time, its transcript and its documents.

## Skipped firings

A firing that cannot or should not go ahead is recorded as **Skipped**, with a
reason, before any work starts. The common ones:

| Reason | What happened |
|---|---|
| **Agent busy** | The agent was already working on another task. An agent works on one task at a time |
| **Previous run active** | This schedule's previous firing was still working |
| **Nothing to process** | The *nothing to process* gate found nothing to do |
| **Outside business hours** | The business-hours gate was closed |
| **Could not check for work** | The *nothing to process* gate could not look |
| **Paused after failures** | The schedule paused itself after repeated failures |
| **Agent not active** | The agent is a draft, paused or archived |
| **No AI credit** | The account has no AI credit to spend. It fires again on its next time once credit is added |
| **Missed window** | The firing's time had already passed |

A skip never piles up work for later: when credit returns or the agent frees
up, the schedule carries on from its **next** time, without replaying the
firings it missed.

**Agent busy**, **Previous run active** and **Nothing to process** are quiet —
they are expected, and send no notification. Every other skip sends one
notification per schedule to the people who can see it.

<Aside type="note">
  When an agent in a task fires one of its *own* schedules, that firing is not
  skipped as busy: it waits and starts as soon as the agent's current reply
  ends.
</Aside>

## Who sees a schedule

Schedules are private to the people involved:

| Person | Sees it | May edit, pause, **Run now** or delete it |
|---|---|---|
| The person who created it | Yes | Yes |
| People it was shared with | Yes, and they take part in every task it starts | No |
| Workspace owners | Yes — owners see every schedule | Yes |
| Anyone else, including admins | No | No |

**Share** on a schedule's page adds people; each one is told with a
notification. The schedule's link opens only for people with access. Anyone
who can see a schedule also sees the tasks it starts.

## From a conversation

You can ask an agent in any task to list, create, change, pause, resume, fire or
delete a schedule — *"check the supplier portal every weekday at 8"*. It uses
the built-in `schedules` [skill](/concepts/skills#built-in-skills), and every
change stops on an approval card first — *The agent wants to create a schedule
"…". Allow it?* — showing when it will fire and the next time. The change is
made as you, the person who started the task, under the same rules as the
Schedules page. A task a schedule started can never change schedules itself.

Two things decide what a person can see and do. Their **role** — owner, admin
or member — decides which *kinds* of action they may take (see
[Users and roles](/administration/users#roles)). **Access** decides which
*things* they see: which workspaces, which agents, which tasks and automations.
This page is about access.

The short version: everything is private to its creator until it is shared, and
an admin role does not unlock anyone else's private work. The one exception is
automations, which a workspace's owners and admins always see. A task a person
started stays theirs: an admin sees it only when it is shared with them. How sharing works —
Can use, Can edit, Everyone in a workspace — is on [Sharing](/concepts/sharing).

## Workspaces and how far a role reaches

Your enterprise holds one or more **workspaces**. A workspace is where the work
lives — its tasks, automations, agents, connectors, skills and the computers
they work on. Owners create them and decide who belongs to each; see
[Workspaces](/administration/workspaces).

| Who | Reaches |
|---|---|
| The owner | Every workspace in the enterprise |
| An admin | The workspaces the owner made them admin of — as an admin there |
| A member | The workspaces they were added to or invited into. May be several |

Nobody gets a workspace by being in the enterprise: an invitation names the
workspace the person joins, and the owner or a workspace admin adds people to
others later. A person in the enterprise who is in no workspace sees *You're not
in a workspace yet* until someone adds them.

<Aside type="note">
  Rights over an agent come from the workspace it belongs to. Being an admin of
  one workspace makes you a member in the others, not an admin of them.
</Aside>

## What owners and admins can open

Opening something by its link is wider than seeing it in a list. A **list** shows
your own items and what is shared with you; an owner's list is no longer a view
of everyone's work.

| Role | Opens by link | Edits |
|---|---|---|
| Member | Own items and what is shared with them | Own items, and where they have Can edit |
| Admin | The same, plus any agent or connector in the workspaces they administer | The same, plus any agent, connector, automation or skill in the workspaces they administer |
| Owner | Anything in any workspace | The same as an admin, in every workspace |
| AptiveAI | Anything in any enterprise | Any agent |

An owner opens someone else's **task** to look, not to work: it is read-only,
and its live view is **view-only** — the screen is visible, but the mouse and
keyboard reach nothing. To work on it, **Continue in my own task** starts a new
task of their own.

**Automations** are the exception to "private until shared": owners and admins
of a workspace see every [automation](/concepts/automations) in it, with what it
costs, and can change, pause, run and delete it.

An admin edits another person's agent in a workspace they do not administer
only if it was shared with them as Can edit.

See [Sharing](/concepts/sharing) for the full rules, who can use and who can
edit each kind, and what happens to a person's shares and automations when they
leave.

## Connectors for personal agents

Each [connector](/concepts/connectors) carries an **Available to personal
agents** switch, which owners and admins turn on or off on the Connectors page.
A personal agent — one not shared with Everyone in a workspace — may only be
granted connectors that have it on, and the rule is checked again every time a
task starts, so turning it off takes the connector away from every personal
agent at its next task. The grants are kept, so turning it back on restores
them.

Leave it off for a connector that reaches something sensitive, and only agents
shared with the whole workspace will be able to use it.

Who can *see and use* a connector is a separate choice, made in its
**Who can use it** share box on the Connectors page — private to whoever added
it until they share it, with **Everyone in ‹workspace›** for owners and admins.
See [Connectors](/concepts/connectors) and [Sharing](/concepts/sharing).
